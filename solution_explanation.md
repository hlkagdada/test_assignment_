# Solution explanation

An expert approach begins by treating the POSCAR and the strain-energy table as two parts of one physical problem. The POSCAR establishes the reference unit cell, its volume, composition, atom count, and therefore the mass density required later for acoustic calculations. The lattice vectors should be interpreted using the POSCAR scaling convention and converted to a Cartesian cell before taking the determinant. The species and multiplicities determine the mass of the cell. These quantities are not merely bookkeeping: a density error propagates directly into every sound velocity and the Debye temperature.

The energy table should then be inspected before fitting. Each strain pattern represents a controlled homogeneous deformation around the reference structure. An expert should verify the required patterns, finite numerical values, duplicate points, and the distribution of positive and negative strain values. The energy minimum need not be assumed to be exactly at a tabulated point. For each independent strain mode, a polynomial fit through fourth order is useful because the desired elastic information is the local quadratic curvature while higher-order terms describe finite-strain anharmonicity. The fit should be evaluated through residuals and goodness-of-fit rather than accepting a coefficient blindly. Comparing positive and negative strain behavior is also useful for detecting asymmetry or numerical contamination. Leave-one-out and deterministic bootstrap calculations provide a reproducible measure of how sensitive the curvature is to individual data points.

The elastic constants must follow the actual crystal symmetry. For an orthorhombic material the stiffness matrix contains nine independent constants: three normal diagonal terms, three shear terms, and three normal-normal coupling terms. The coupling constants require the combined normal-strain curves; they cannot be recovered by imposing cubic symmetry. The energy-curvature convention and the engineering-shear Voigt convention must be handled consistently. After converting the curvature from energy per cell to energy density and then from eV per cubic angstrom to GPa, the complete symmetric 6x6 stiffness matrix can be assembled. An expert should explicitly check its eigenvalues and the orthorhombic mechanical-stability conditions. Inverting the matrix gives the compliance matrix, and multiplying stiffness by compliance provides an independent numerical consistency check.

Aggregate polycrystalline properties should be obtained from both the stiffness and compliance representations. Voigt estimates use direct stiffness components, while Reuss estimates use the compliance matrix. Hill averages are the arithmetic means of the corresponding Voigt and Reuss quantities. From the Hill bulk and shear moduli, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, and the Pugh ratio follow. The universal anisotropy index provides another measure of departure from isotropic behavior. These quantities should be checked against the Voigt/Reuss bounds rather than reported without validation.

The directional part of the calculation requires retaining the full orthorhombic tensor. For a unit direction vector, the compliance tensor gives the directional Young's modulus, while the stiffness/compliance contractions can be used for directional compressibility. Sampling the sphere should be sufficiently dense and deterministic, with the extrema reported together with their directions. The purpose is not merely to create a plot but to demonstrate that the anisotropy follows from the recovered tensor rather than from an isotropic approximation.

For acoustic properties, the Christoffel matrix is constructed from the fourth-rank elastic tensor and the propagation direction. Its three eigenvalues correspond to density-weighted squared acoustic velocities. The eigenvectors distinguish the longitudinal and transverse modes, and the smallest and largest velocities over sampled directions quantify acoustic anisotropy. Separately, isotropic longitudinal and transverse velocities can be derived from the Hill bulk and shear moduli. Their combination gives a mean sound velocity, which together with the cell volume, atom count, and physical constants determines a Debye temperature.

Finally, robustness should be assessed rather than treating one numerical fit as exact. The deterministic bootstrap curvature samples can be propagated through the complete elastic-property calculation, allowing uncertainty estimates for the stiffness-derived quantities and identifying the most sensitive elastic constant or derived observable. The final JSON should contain only values generated from the supplied raw inputs and deterministic algorithms. No reference numbers should be required by the candidate, and the verifier should independently recompute scientific quantities from the raw files so that merely creating a correctly shaped output or hard-coding expected answers cannot pass.
